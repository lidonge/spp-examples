Test the language
against real workflows.

Two demonstrations put S++ under different kinds of pressure: an end-to-end commerce journey and deposit transactions reconstructed from a banking legacy system.

01 / PETSTORE COMMERCE

A complete customer journey

The Petstore demonstration spans customer, product, inventory, and shop domains. It covers buyer registration, catalogue browsing, carts, order placement, payment, shipment, delivery, cancellation, return, and reservation. APP definitions can deploy the domains together, while the scenes keep their business participants and transaction boundaries explicit.

What it tests

  • Entities playing domain roles
  • Checks, calculations, and quantum updates
  • Explicit transaction and failure flows
  • Role facts retained as order-time snapshots

Read the flow

Start at the payment scene: a buyer, merchant, order, and payment agreement participate. The scene checks eligibility before the bounded funds transfer and records the paid order and receipt.

02 / DEPOSIT SYSTEM BANKING

Account opening and cash deposit

The deposit demonstration studies selected transactions from an existing COBOL banking system. It models account opening and cash deposit alongside ledger postings, overdraft repayment allocation, and reversal behavior. The goal is to test whether the business rules can be made explicit while generated Java handles the chosen implementation.

This is a focused conversion exercise, not a claim that the complete banking application has been migrated.

What it tests

  • Explicit agreement and participant roles
  • Quantum updates for financial amounts
  • Transaction boundaries and reverse operations
  • Comparison with legacy business branches

Why it matters

Banking workflows reveal where a declarative business model needs precise rules for ordering, rollback, reversal, and compensation. Open design questions are recorded with the demonstration rather than hidden in handwritten Java.
