Business behavior,

S++ (ServPP) is an experimental role-oriented language for business systems. It describes who participates in a business operation, what must be checked, what changes, and where consistency matters. The implementation can adapt without losing the business story.

THE PROBLEM 01

Business meaning gets buried
in implementation.

In a conventional system, a single business operation may be scattered across models, controllers, validation, persistence, and transaction code. S++ brings the participants and the operation back into view.

THE LANGUAGE 02
01

Model the role, not just the object.

A person can act as a buyer or a merchant. A product can become goods, then an order line. Each role makes its own business facts visible.

↗
02

Make every scenario accountable.

A scene declares its participants and calls checks, calculations, and updates in business order. Transaction boundaries are visible in the source.

↗
03

Keep deployment decisions movable.

The application and physical layers choose service routing, storage, numeric representation, and transaction implementation.

IN THE SOURCE 03

The business operation
is readable at a glance.

Here is an excerpt from the Petstore payment scene. The participants, eligibility checks, bounded funds transfer, and record updates are all part of one explicit flow.

Follow the Petstore flow →
petshop-scene-payShopOrder.sppSPP
Scenario serial payShopOrder(
    Buyer, Merchant, ShopOrder, PaymentAgreement
) {
    checkBuyerCredential;
    checkPaymentEligibility;
    preparePayment;

    transaction {
        payOrderFunds bounds {
            Buyer.wallet: [0, null];
            Merchant.cash: [0, null];
        };
        updateShopOrder;
        insertReceipt(PaymentAgreement.receipt);
    }
}

Illustrative excerpt; omitted preparation and lookup steps are present in the demo source.
